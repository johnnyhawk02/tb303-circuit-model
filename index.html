How the model works

Pitch. The 6-bit latch drives a 100k/200k ladder from 5.333 V, so each code step is exactly 1/12 V. The ladder is solved by Thevenin reduction, so a mismatched resistor detunes the notes it feeds, as on the board.

VCO. An exponential current charges C33 from 5.5 V to 12 V, then Q27/Q28 reset it. The reset is placed with sub-sample accuracy and smoothed with a polyBLEP so high notes stay clean.

Square. The ramp is coupled through C10 into the base of Q8. Because that coupling is a high-pass, the duty cycle narrows on low notes, which is part of the original's character.

VCF. Four coupled capacitor voltages, one per ladder stage, with a tanh law for each transistor pair. The bottom cap is 18n against 33n above it. Feedback through VR4 is high-passed by C15, so resonance thins out at low cutoff settings. Full feedback (k = 17) is the point of self-oscillation.

Envelopes. The filter envelope charges C62 and decays through R138 + VR6, which gives the 200 ms to 2.5 s range printed on the sheet. On accented notes the 4066 shorts VR6, so decay drops to minimum, and the envelope also charges the accent sweep cap through R117. Consecutive accents build up on that cap.

VCA and resonance compensation. The filter feeds the OTA through C49/R88, and the resonance pot wiper is mixed in through C76/R100. That second path restores much of the bass the ladder loses as feedback rises (its passband gain is 1/(1+k)). The VCA envelope is C67 charged through R49 and decaying through R52.

Mods. Parts marked mod are the ones people usually change on 303s and TD-3s. Most map to Devil Fish controls (Robin Whittle's well-known rebuild): Normal and Accent decay, VEG decay, Soft attack, Slide time, Sweep speed, and resonance raised to self-oscillation.
